## Codex Community Hackathon – Guidelines (Extracted Content)

### Overview

* Open-theme, solo hackathon focused on creativity, rapid development, and AI-assisted software building.
* Participants may build any product, tool, software solution, or prototype.
* The goal is to turn an idea into a functional project within the hackathon duration using Codex/OpenAI tools whenever useful.

---

### Participation Format

* This is a solo hackathon.
* Each participant must build and submit individually.
* Team submissions are not allowed.

---

### Codex / AI Usage

* The hackathon is designed around AI-assisted software development.
* Participants are expected to use Codex/OpenAI tools meaningfully during the build process.
* AI tools may be used for:

  * Ideation
  * Architecture design
  * Coding
  * Debugging
  * Testing
  * Documentation
  * Deployment support
* Participants must document how Codex/OpenAI or other AI tools were used.
* Participants should be able to explain:

  * Core functionality
  * Architecture
  * AI-assisted development process

---

### Rules

* All projects must be built during the hackathon.
* Pre-existing completed projects or reused submissions are not allowed.
* Starter templates, boilerplates, open-source libraries, APIs, and public datasets are permitted.
* The core product, major features, and final implementation must be created during the hackathon.
* OpenAI API credits and Codex access/support will be provided where applicable.
* Plagiarism, copied projects, or misrepresentation may lead to disqualification.

---

### Registration & Entry

* Participants must present the Luma QR code received in the registration approval email.
* Entry will not be permitted without a valid registration approval email.
* Participants should carry:

  * Laptop
  * Charger
  * Required accessories

---

### Timeline

* **Date:** 6 June 2026 – 7 June 2026
* **Start Time:** 8:00 PM, 6 June
* **End Time:** Around 8:00 AM, 7 June
* **Format:** Overnight hackathon
* **Duration:** Approximately 12 hours

---

### Judging & Submission Format

* This is a submission-based hackathon.
* There will be no live judging/demo round at the venue.
* Participants must submit all required materials before the submission deadline.
* Judges will review submissions after the hackathon based on the submitted materials.

---

### Submission Requirements

#### 1. Demo

* Recorded demo or pitch video showing the working software/prototype.

#### 2. Source Code

* Source code repository (preferably GitHub or GitLab).

#### 3. Project Description

Must include:

* Problem statement
* Solution overview
* Tech stack used
* Codex/OpenAI or other AI usage

#### 4. Optional

* Pitch deck (maximum 10 slides)

---

### Evaluation Focus

Judges will evaluate based on:

1. Functionality of the prototype
2. Creativity and originality
3. Practical usefulness and impact
4. Technical soundness
5. Clarity of submission and demo video
6. Effective use of Codex/OpenAI tools
7. Ability to build and iterate within the hackathon duration

---

### Code of Conduct

* Maintain professional and respectful behavior throughout the event.
* Respect the venue, organizers, mentors, and fellow participants.
* Misconduct, harassment, plagiarism, or rule violations may result in disqualification.
* Participants are expected to follow organizer instructions during the overnight event.

---

### General Guidance

* Focus on a functional prototype rather than a perfect product.
* Keep the demo simple and understandable.
* Prioritize clarity, feasibility, and impact.
* Manage time effectively during the overnight build.
* Properly document all AI usage in the final submission.

---

## Key Strategic Takeaways

Since this is:

* **Solo**
* **12 hours only**
* **Submission-based (no live demo pressure)**
* **Judged heavily on usefulness, functionality, and Codex usage**

The winning strategy is usually:

1. Pick a focused problem.
2. Build one feature extremely well.
3. Use Codex heavily and document its contribution.
4. Record a polished demo video.
5. Submit a clean GitHub repository with good documentation.
6. Avoid attempting a large startup-scale product in 12 hours.

For your background, something in **AI developer tooling, agent observability, evaluation systems, productivity automation, or developer infrastructure** would likely fit better than trying to build a full ML model from scratch overnight.
